No housing officer wants to place a family two hours from their school, their GP and their grandmother. It happens anyway, thousands of times a year, and almost never because anyone decided it was a good idea.
{: .lead-in}

## The scale of it

Out-of-area placements have become routine rather than exceptional. At the end of June 2025, **81% of all out-of-area arrangements in England** were households that had applied to London authorities: 34,280 of 42,080.

In London itself, more than **34,000 households were placed outside their home borough during 2025**, up more than 40% in two years and more than double the figure of a decade ago.

The concentration in London is not because London officers care less. It is because the gap between what households can afford, what Local Housing Allowance covers, and what the local market charges is widest there. When nothing suitable exists inside the boundary at a price the authority can pay, the search moves outward. Distance is what a shortage looks like on a map.

## What it costs the household

The financial figures get quoted more often, so it is worth being specific about the rest.

A child moved out of borough often changes school, or keeps the old one and absorbs a long commute that eats into homework, sleep and friendships. Either way, attainment tends to suffer. A parent may lose a job that depended on being nearby, or on childcare from family who are now unreachable. Healthcare gets interrupted at exactly the point people are least able to advocate for themselves, re-registering with a GP, re-referring into mental health services, restarting a waiting list from the beginning.

Support networks are the part that rarely appears in any dataset. The neighbour who watches the kids for an hour, the relative who lends twenty pounds before payday: these are what keep marginal households from tipping into crisis. Move someone forty miles and that infrastructure is gone, without anything replacing it.

## What it costs the authority

The placing authority keeps the duty regardless of geography, and discharging it at distance is harder and more expensive in every direction.

Officers travel to inspect. Reviews take longer. Conditions are harder to monitor, and problems surface later and larger. Communication with the household degrades, which raises the chance of a placement breaking down and the whole process restarting. Receiving authorities absorb pressure on their own services with no say in the matter and no funding attached, which is why relations between neighbouring authorities can sour over placements neither of them wanted.

And out-of-area placements are more likely to be nightly-paid, which is the most expensive form of temporary accommodation there is. The distance and the cost travel together.

## Why it keeps happening

Three things drive it, and only one is really about supply.

**Genuine shortage.** In parts of the country there simply are not enough homes at the price point. National policy on social housing and LHA rates is the answer here, and no platform changes that.

**Time pressure.** A household presenting today needs somewhere tonight. The search radius expands with every hour that passes without a suitable option. Many out-of-area placements are not the best available answer. They are the best answer findable in the time available.

**Invisibility.** This is the one that gets least attention. Suitable, compliant, willing landlords frequently exist within the boundary, and the authority has no way of seeing them. They are not on anyone's list. They have never been approached. The stock is not missing; it is unmatched.

The second and third are the same problem wearing different clothes. Both are about what a housing officer can see, and how fast.

## What actually reduces it

Nothing here substitutes for building more homes. But within the constraints teams are working under today:

- **Widen the pool of known local landlords.** Every accredited landlord inside the boundary who has never been approached is a placement that could have stayed local.
- **Cut the time between presentation and offer.** Speed is the difference between a considered local placement and an emergency booking somewhere far away.
- **Keep compliance current, not checked on demand.** Verifying certificates during a placement adds days. Verifying them continuously adds none.
- **Track distance as a measure in its own right.** Teams that report on out-of-area rates alongside cost tend to reduce them. What gets measured gets managed.
